If you already live legally in Spain, the next logical step is to bring your loved ones over. That is what family reunification (reagrupación familiar) is for. Health cover for every arriving relative is one of the mandatory conditions — but it is met in different ways: for some families the public system is enough, others need a private policy for each person. Here is how to tell which case is yours, and what the paperwork is checked for.

## What family reunification is

Reagrupación familiar allows a Spanish resident to bring their family to join them. Usually this means:

- a spouse or registered partner;
- minor children (your own or under guardianship);
- dependent parents, under certain conditions.

The resident applicant proves sufficient income, housing, legal status and health cover for those arriving. The process has two stages: the resident first obtains authorisation from the Oficina de Extranjería, and only then does the relative apply for a visa at the Spanish consulate in their own country.

## Public healthcare or a private policy

The requirement is phrased as "health cover", and there are two ways to meet it:

- **Through Seguridad Social.** If the sponsoring resident works and pays contributions, arriving relatives are usually registered as their *beneficiarios* and get access to the public system. A separate private policy is often not needed in that case.
- **Through a private policy.** If the resident lives in Spain without paying into the system — on a [non-lucrative visa](/en/blog/non-lucrative-visa-insurance/), for instance — the family has no public cover, and each arriving relative needs a private health policy.

What each system actually gives you is set out in our comparison of [public and private healthcare in Spain](/en/blog/private-vs-public-healthcare-spain/).

Two caveats explain why families take a private policy even when the public route would formally do. First, *beneficiario* status is arranged after arrival, and time passes between entry and holding a *tarjeta sanitaria* — a policy bridges that gap. Second, the document list is set by the particular Oficina de Extranjería, and the visa stage by the consulate; their requirements differ. Check against the current list published by the office you are actually filing with, rather than a general description of the procedure.

## What the policy has to cover

Where a private policy is needed, the conditions are the same as for any [residency insurance](/en/blog/insurance-for-residency-visa/):

- **full cover with no copays** (*copago* — a patient fee charged per visit; see [with or without copays](/en/blog/copay-or-no-copay/));
- **no waiting periods** (*carencia* — a period after signing during which some treatments are not yet covered; see [waiting periods](/en/blog/carencias-waiting-periods-spain/)) — cover from day one;
- **for a full year** and across the whole of Spain;
- **with a certificate** for immigration and the consulate.

## When to buy the policy, and from what date

Reunification runs over months, so "is it too early to buy insurance?" comes up almost every time. The practical markers:

- Residency policies can usually be arranged **around 150 days before arrival** — well before the visa appointment.
- The certificate normally arrives as a PDF straight after payment, so it is in hand when the file is submitted.
- **Set the start date to the planned entry**, not to the day of purchase: a policy that expires before your relative reaches Spain has to be redone.
- The first payment usually goes through on an international card, but from the second year it is typically taken from a Spanish account (IBAN). It is worth opening one early for the relative who arrives.

## What is checked on each relative's certificate

The authorities do not read the whole policy — they read the [insurance certificate](/en/blog/insurance-certificate-for-visa/), one per arriving person. Check yours against this table before filing:

| What the certificate must show | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Full name and passport number of each relative | the details must match the application, or the file comes back for correction |
| A separate certificate for each person, children included | a single "family" document with no names listed is usually not accepted |
| Start and end dates covering the whole period | a policy shorter than the authorisation will not do |
| The wording "sin copagos" | a policy with patient fees does not meet the requirement |
| The wording "sin carencias" | cover has to be effective from day one |
| Territory — the whole of Spain | an island-only policy will not do |
| An insurer licensed to operate in Spain | a policy bought in the country of origin is generally not accepted for this procedure |

## Particularities for the family

- **Cover for everyone.** It is needed for all arrivals — adults and children alike.
- **Family policy.** It is often more convenient to include everyone under a single family policy: easier to manage and usually better value than separate ones. More on this in our piece on [family insurance in Tenerife](/en/blog/family-insurance-tenerife/).
- **Children.** For children, paediatrics and prevention matter — worth checking they are included.
- **Dependent parents.** Reuniting with parents is allowed under extra conditions (age, proven dependency), and policies for older relatives deserve a closer read: this is where limits on [pre-existing conditions](/en/blog/preexisting-conditions-insurance-spain/) most often surface.

> Tip: arrange the policies early so the certificates for the whole family are ready to go in as one set. Mismatched dates and a missing certificate for a child are the most common reasons a file is handed back.

## If the application is refused

Refusals happen, and the money already spent on policies is a fair concern. Residency policies are generally non-refundable when the customer simply changes their mind, but **an official refusal of the application is usually written in as a specific exception**: the policy is cancelled on presentation of the decision. The wording varies from product to product, so find it in the terms **before** you pay, not after a refusal.

## Common mistakes

- **Taking a travel policy** — it will not be accepted for reunification.
- **Not covering everyone** — a certificate is needed for each relative, children included.
- **Saving on a policy with copays** — the application may be refused.
- **Setting the start date to the purchase date** — the policy can expire before arrival.
- **Assuming the sponsor's Seguridad Social contributions settle it automatically** — *beneficiario* status is registered separately, and only after arrival.
