Tenerife attracts not only people moving for good but also students: universities, language schools, exchange programmes. If you are coming to study for more than three months, you will need a student visa — and with it, the right health insurance. Here is what the consulate actually checks and where students most often slip up.

## When you need a student visa

A student visa is required to study in Spain for **more than 90 days** — whether at a university, college or on a long language course. One of the mandatory documents is a private health insurance policy.

## The insurance requirement

For a student visa the consulate expects a policy meeting the same strict conditions as [insurance for a residency visa](/en/blog/insurance-for-residency-visa/):

- **full cover with no co-payments** (*copago* — a fee you pay per visit; more in [copay or no copay](/en/blog/copay-or-no-copay/));
- **no waiting periods** (*carencia* — the time after purchase when some services are not yet covered; see [waiting periods](/en/blog/carencias-waiting-periods-spain/)) — cover from day one;
- **for the whole study period** and across all of Spain;
- **with a certificate for the consulate** confirming these terms.

## What the certificate must show: a checklist

The consulate reads the [insurance certificate](/en/blog/insurance-certificate-for-visa/), not the whole policy. Check yours against this table before you apply:

| What the certificate must show | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Full name and passport number exactly as on the visa application | details must match the passport, or the file comes back for correction |
| Start and end dates covering your entire course | a policy shorter than the programme is not accepted |
| The wording «sin copagos» | a policy with co-payments does not meet the requirement |
| The wording «sin carencias» | cover must apply from the first day |
| Territory: the whole of Spain | an island-only policy will not do |
| Doctors, emergencies, hospital care, repatriation | the consulate wants full medical cover, not just travel assistance |

Student policies usually issue the certificate as a PDF straight after payment, so you will not be waiting weeks for it.

## How student policies work

- **A term of 2 to 12 months.** The policy runs for your studies, but for no more than a year.
- **Usually not renewable.** These plans are typically sold as a single term. If your course continues, you take out a new policy for the next year.
- **Paid by card.** Student plans are normally paid by debit or credit card — no Spanish bank account needed.
- **Language courses.** If the course is longer than 90 days and you apply for a student visa, the requirements are the same as for university study.

## Studying longer than a year, or staying on afterwards

A two-year master's, renewing your student card or switching to another type of residence are all common. Since a student policy is single-term, a new period needs a new policy. If you change status — to residence on other grounds, say — a resident plan with the same terms (no co-payments, no waiting periods) fits. Bear in mind that from the second year these plans usually collect premiums from a Spanish bank account (IBAN), so it pays to open one early.

## Travel insurance: when it is enough

| Situation | Which policy |
|---|---|
| A course or internship of up to 90 days | a study travel policy — medical assistance while abroad, repatriation, baggage |
| Studying longer than 90 days on a student visa | a health policy with no co-payments or waiting periods, with a certificate |

A study travel policy suits a short trip, but it will not be accepted for a long-stay visa: it is built for assistance while travelling, not for ongoing treatment. More in our guide to [travel insurance for coming to Spain](/en/blog/travel-insurance-coming-to-spain/).

> Tip: set the policy start date to your arrival or course start, but buy it well ahead so the certificate is in hand when you apply. A late certificate is one of the most common reasons for delays.

## Common mistakes

- **Taking a travel policy** — it will not be accepted for a student visa.
- **Saving on a policy with co-payments** — the application may be refused.
- **Counting on renewing the student policy** — it is usually single-term; next year you need a new one.
- **Leaving it to the last minute** — sort it out early.
