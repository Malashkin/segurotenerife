Tenerife looks like an easy destination: a European island, four hours from London, a hospital in every large town. That is exactly why people fly in with no cover at all, or with a policy bought in thirty seconds that excludes the one thing they came for. The questions below are the ones worth answering before you book.

## Is Tenerife classed as Spain for travel insurance?

**Yes.** Tenerife is part of the Canary Islands, an autonomous region of Spain, and a policy that covers Spain covers Tenerife.

The nuance that makes people ask is tax, not geography. The Canaries are an outermost region of the EU with a special status — they sit outside the EU VAT area and have their own local tax instead, which is why duty-free rules differ when you fly to the mainland. That has nothing to do with your medical cover, but some insurers write their geographical zones in their own way and list islands separately. So: **check how the zone is worded in your own contract**, and if the Canaries are named as a separate zone, make sure yours includes them.

## Is Tenerife in Europe for travel insurance?

**Yes.** It is a region of an EU member state, and every insurer that offers a "Europe" zone includes Spain in it. Geographically the island sits off the coast of Africa, which is where the doubt comes from — but for insurance purposes the country matters, not the latitude.

## EHIC and GHIC: what the card does and does not do

A European Health Insurance Card, or the UK's Global Health Insurance Card, gives you **medically necessary state healthcare on the same terms as a Spanish resident**. Break an arm, and the public hospital treats you as it would a local, which in Spain means the treatment itself is covered.

What the card does not pay for:

- **medical repatriation** — a flight home with medical escort, the single most expensive item on this list;
- **search and rescue** — evacuation from a trail or a helicopter lift;
- **private clinics**, and in the tourist south of Tenerife a great many clinics are private;
- **lost baggage** and delayed luggage;
- **cancelling or cutting short the trip**.

That is the whole argument. The card is a useful thing to carry and it is not a substitute for a policy — the two cover different problems.

## What a policy should cover on Tenerife specifically

**Medical repatriation.** You are on an island. Any evacuation means a flight, and a medical flight from the Canaries to northern Europe is not comparable in price to an ambulance ride within one country.

**The activities you are actually here for.** The Teide ascent and the trails of Anaga and Masca, diving off Los Cristianos, surfing at El Médano, volcano excursions. Many basic policies list mountain trekking above a certain altitude and water sports under **exclusions** — read that section, not the sales page. More detail: [active sport in Tenerife and what insurance covers](/en/blog/active-sports-insurance-tenerife/).

**A hire car.** Almost everyone rents on Tenerife. The rental contract leaves you liable for an excess (*franquicia*), and whether your travel policy has a car hire excess option is worth asking before you sign at the desk.

**Cruises.** Santa Cruz is a major cruise port, and a cruise adds risks a standard travel policy does not price — see [cruise cover](/en/blog/cruise-travel-insurance-spain/).

> Read the exclusions first and the cover list second. A policy is defined by what it refuses to pay for.

## You live in Tenerife and are travelling somewhere else

Different situation, different policy. A resident of Spain flying out several times a year — to see family, for work on the mainland, on holiday — is usually better served by an [annual multi-trip policy](/en/blog/annual-multi-trip-insurance-spain/) than by buying single-trip cover each time. Watch the maximum duration per trip: that is the clause that catches people out on long absences.

## Travel insurance is not a residency policy

If you are moving rather than visiting, a travel policy will not do the job. Residency and visa applications require a full health policy with no copayments and no waiting periods, plus a certificate for the consulate — the details are in [insurance for a residency visa](/en/blog/insurance-for-residency-visa/) and [the insurance certificate for a visa](/en/blog/insurance-certificate-for-visa/).
