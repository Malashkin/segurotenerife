People come to Tenerife for exactly this: the waves at El Médano, diving off Los Cristianos, a 3,718-metre volcano and the trails of Anaga. And almost nobody checks whether their insurance covers the very thing they came for.

## Why this is a topic of its own

Most policies have an exclusions section, and it almost always contains wording about **hazardous sports**. The reasoning is straightforward: the insurer priced your risk as "ordinary life", and a paraglider launch was not part of that assessment.

A typical exclusions list:

- scuba diving;
- surfing, kitesurfing, windsurfing;
- climbing and trekking above a certain altitude;
- air sports — paragliding, skydiving;
- motorsport and downhill mountain biking.

The exact list is always individual. What you need to read is not the marketing page but the **exclusiones** section.

## What happens in practice

A surfing injury is not an abstraction: torn ligaments, a fracture, a shoulder injury from the board. Then medical treatment starts, and that is when you find out whether it is covered.

The worst case is the mountains. **Evacuation from a route** on Teide or in the Masca gorge is a rescue operation, sometimes by helicopter. The cost of such an operation is the single most expensive line in the whole story — and it is precisely the one most often left uncovered.

> Look for two words in the policy: **rescate** (rescue and evacuation) and **deportes de riesgo**. The first present, the second absent from the exclusions, is what you want.

## The options

**Extending a travel policy.** There are travel policies with hazardous sports cover built in — usually taken by students and exchange programme participants, but they suit anyone travelling for the activity.

**[Accident insurance](/en/blog/accident-insurance-spain/).** This is about the injury itself: payouts for disability, compensation, support after the event. Useful if you do sport regularly rather than once a holiday.

**A [health policy](/en/blog/private-healthcare-tenerife/) for living on the island.** If you live here, treating an injury runs through it — but check how it treats sports injuries and whether there are limits.

**A [hospital cash policy](/en/blog/hospitalization-cash-insurance-spain/).** A serious injury means weeks off work. That is exactly what it compensates.

## What to check before the season

1. **Is your sport named explicitly?** "Water sports" and "surfing" are not always the same thing in policy wording.
2. **Amateur or competitive.** Competition is excluded more often than doing it for yourself.
3. **Evacuation and search** — covered, and to what limit.
4. **Diving depth.** Policies often set a limit beyond which there is no cover.
5. **Equipment** — is damage or loss compensated.

## A note on Teide

Reaching the summit crater requires a **permit**, arranged in advance and free of charge, but with limited places. Insurance does not replace the permit — they are different things. Evacuation from the route, however, is yours to pay for if it is not in the policy.
