Children's dentistry works differently from adults': treatment is frequent, planned and heavily age-dependent. Parents who have recently moved to Tenerife rarely find it obvious what the state covers and what they need to organise themselves.

## What public healthcare provides

If your child is registered with the public system, **children's dental programmes** are available: check-ups, prevention, treatment of milk and permanent teeth within certain ages. Scope and age limits are set at community level, so it is worth confirming the Canary terms with your paediatrician.

What is almost never included: **orthodontics** — braces, plates, aligners. That is the most expensive part of children's dentistry, and it is paid separately.

How the two tiers of medicine relate is covered in [private versus public healthcare](/en/blog/private-vs-public-healthcare-spain/).

## What a dental policy adds for a child

**At no charge:** regular check-ups, X-rays, prevention, fissure sealing, emergency care for a knocked tooth (a realistic scenario with an active child).

**On a fixed price list:** complex decay treatment, orthodontics, surgery.

The value of a child's policy is primarily **preventive**: check-ups with nothing to pay on the day mean you bring the child every six months rather than "when it hurts". That is exactly how expensive treatment is avoided.

## Planning orthodontics

Braces are not an emergency. An orthodontist normally indicates the likely starting age a year or two ahead. That is an ideal situation for insurance:

- the policy is taken out **in advance** and calmly clears its [waiting period](/en/blog/carencias-waiting-periods-spain/);
- by the time treatment starts you are on the insurer's price list;
- treatment runs for a long time, and a predictable price across the whole period is half the peace of mind.

> The expensive mistake: visiting the orthodontist, getting a treatment plan and only then looking for insurance. By that point the waiting period has closed off most of the good options.

## Why a family policy

Families with children usually have a separate construction available: **a single price for several insured people** — parents plus children, sometimes the older generation too. It costs less than individual policies for each and is easier to administer: one renewal date, one insurer, one clinic network.

The same principle applies to health cover — see [family insurance in Tenerife](/en/blog/family-insurance-tenerife/).

## What to look at

1. **Whether children's items** appear in the no-charge list.
2. **The orthodontics price list** — ask to see it before buying.
3. **Clinics near home or school** — you will not drive across the island with a child.
4. **A dentist who speaks your language**, if the child's Spanish is not there yet.
5. **The family tariff terms** — how many people are included and whether there is a supplement from the third insured onwards.
