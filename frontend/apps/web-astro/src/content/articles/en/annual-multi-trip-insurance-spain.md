Island life means flying. Visiting parents, a work trip to the mainland, a holiday, a friend's wedding — and every time you arrange insurance again, pay again and read the terms again. There is a simpler way.

## What a multi-trip policy is

Travel insurance bought **for a year and for every trip at once**. You pay once, then simply travel: each journey is automatically covered, with nothing to arrange separately.

Cover is standard for a travel policy:

- **medical assistance** abroad, with a solid limit — up to 150,000 € in the popular variants;
- **repatriation and medical transport**;
- **trip cancellation** — compensation for non-refundable costs;
- **baggage** — loss, delay, damage.

These policies generally carry no waiting periods: cover works immediately.

## When it beats single-trip cover

Simple arithmetic: one week's cover costs little, but four single policies in a year already approach the price of an annual one. **As a guide, from three or four trips a year.**

The break-even point depends on where and how long you fly, so price it against your own schedule rather than a general rule.

Who it usually suits:

- **people who regularly fly home** — to family, for holidays, on business;
- **remote workers with trips** — see also [insurance for autónomos](/en/blog/autonomo-business-insurance/);
- **families** who manage a holiday, a mainland trip and a visit to relatives within a year;
- **spontaneous travellers** — no need to remember about insurance every time.

> An underrated benefit: you stop flying uninsured. Single-trip cover is often simply forgotten before a short journey — and that is the journey where things happen.

## What to check when choosing

1. **Maximum length of a single trip.** The key parameter. An annual policy covers many trips, but each within a set duration.
2. **Geography.** Worldwide or by zone; trips to the USA and Canada are often rated separately.
3. **The medical limit** and whether repatriation is included.
4. **Cancellation** — what counts as a valid reason.
5. **Sport.** If you ski or dive on your trips you need a version with [active sports cover](/en/blog/active-sports-insurance-tenerife/).
6. **Age limits** — not present on every variant, but worth checking.

## What it is not

A multi-trip policy is **insurance for travel**, not a health policy for living in Spain. It will not do:

- for a [visa or residence permit](/en/blog/insurance-for-residency-visa/) — that needs a health policy with a [certificate](/en/blog/insurance-certificate-for-visa/);
- as a replacement for [ongoing health cover](/en/blog/private-healthcare-tenerife/) on the island — at home you are treated under that, not under a travel policy.

If instead you are only about to arrive in Spain, what you need is a [policy for new arrivals](/en/blog/travel-insurance-coming-to-spain/).
