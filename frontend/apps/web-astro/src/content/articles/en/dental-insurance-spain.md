Looking after your teeth at a private clinic in Tenerife can get expensive if you pay for every visit separately. Dental insurance flips that logic: prevention becomes free, and complex treatment comes at reduced rates you know in advance. Let us walk through how it works, in plain terms.

## Why dental is counted separately

Public healthcare in Spain (Seguridad Social) barely covers dentistry — only extractions and emergencies. Everything else is private clinics. That is why dental cover exists either as its own product or as part of a fuller health policy.

## What is usually included at no extra cost

In most dental programmes the free part is exactly prevention and diagnostics — the things that cost the most "à la carte" if you go regularly:

- check-up and dentist consultation;
- professional cleaning (hygiene);
- X-rays;
- emergency care for pain;
- treatment plan and specialist consultations.

## What has a copay — but at a reduced price

Complex treatment is paid for, but at the insurer's rates rather than the full private price:

- fillings and root canals;
- crowns, bridges, dentures;
- implants;
- orthodontics (braces, aligners).

The point is that the price is known upfront and fixed — you do not depend on a particular clinic's list.

## Dental care and the residency policy

If you take out a full health policy for residency, basic dental care is often already included. A separate dental policy makes sense for those planning serious treatment — implants, orthodontics, prosthetics — who want to fix the cost in advance.

> Tip: if you know big treatment is ahead, choose a policy where those procedures come at a reduced rate and without a long waiting period.

## How much it costs

Dental programmes are usually inexpensive per month, and the savings on prevention and complex treatment cover the cost. The exact amount is calculated individually — it depends on age, family and whether you need dental care separately or inside a health policy.
