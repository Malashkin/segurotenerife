Tenerife is a major cruise port: some Atlantic and Canary itineraries start right in Santa Cruz. And if you are boarding a ship, it is worth understanding that a cruise is not an ordinary trip and carries its own risks.

## How a cruise differs from a normal trip

**The medical centre on board is chargeable.** There is a doctor on the ship, but it is a private medical service. Consultations and treatment are billed to your cabin at rates closer to a private clinic. There is no "insurance by default" there.

**Evacuation is expensive.** In a serious case the ship does not turn back — a medical evacuation is organised to the nearest port or by helicopter. That is one of the most expensive items in the whole of insurance.

**The ship does not wait.** Miss the departure at a port of call and you catch it up at the next port, at your own expense.

**Several countries in one trip.** An itinerary around the Canaries, Madeira and Morocco already means multiple jurisdictions and different health systems.

## What the policy must include

1. **Medical care on board** — with a sufficient limit, because "on board" is expensive.
2. **Medical evacuation and repatriation** — the key item; without it a cruise policy is pointless.
3. **Cancellation** before departure — cruises are booked far ahead and paid in full, in non-refundable amounts.
4. **Missed departure** — the cost of catching the ship up.
5. **Baggage** — including delayed delivery to the ship.
6. **Cover for every country on the itinerary**, not just the country of departure.

> Check the geography against the actual route. A "Canaries" cruise may call at Morocco or Madeira — and that is already outside the EU.

## Who this matters to in Tenerife

Two different groups:

- **island residents** setting out on a cruise from Santa Cruz — for you this is a trip, and your [ordinary health policy](/en/blog/private-healthcare-tenerife/) does not work on board;
- **visitors** who flew in, spent time on the island and are boarding a ship — they need cover for the whole chain, not just the hotel days.

If the cruise is not your only trip this year, look at an [annual multi-trip policy](/en/blog/annual-multi-trip-insurance-spain/) — it sometimes covers the cruise and everything else for less.

## Common misconceptions

**"I have health insurance in Spain, that is enough."** It is not: a health policy is for living somewhere, not for travelling. The difference is set out in the article on [arriving in Spain](/en/blog/travel-insurance-coming-to-spain/).

**"The cruise line already included something."** Usually the bare minimum, and only for certain events. Read what exactly.

**"I am healthy, nothing will happen."** The most common claim on a cruise is not a catastrophe but ordinary food poisoning, an injury on an excursion or a chronic condition flaring up far from shore.
