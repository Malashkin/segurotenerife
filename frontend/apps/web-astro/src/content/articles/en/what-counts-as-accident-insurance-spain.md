"But I have accident insurance" is a sentence that sounds confident right up to the moment it turns out that what happened does not count as an accident. The definition is worth understanding, because it decides everything.

## The four tests

In Spanish insurance law an accident is an event meeting four criteria at once:

1. **An external cause.** The cause is outside the body, not within it.
2. **Suddenness.** It happened in an instant rather than developing over years.
3. **Unintentional.** Not by your own will.
4. **Bodily injury.** There is physical harm to health.

One missing criterion is enough for the event to fall outside cover.

Falling down the stairs: external, sudden, unintentional, harm done — an accident. Chronic back pain from years of desk work: internal, gradual — not.

## Why a heart attack usually does not count

A heart attack is an **internal** event. There is no external cause, so the first test fails. That is why a standard accident policy does not cover a heart attack, however sudden it felt to the person.

This is one of the most painful surprises: people are convinced they are insured "in case something serious happens", and the most likely serious event is not covered.

**But extended versions exist.** The range includes policies where **acute myocardial infarction is expressly treated as an accident** by a specific contract term. That is not a general rule but a particular option on a particular product — and it has to be found in the wording rather than assumed.

> If cardiac risk is what matters to you, look for explicit wording about infarction in the conditions. Extended versions normally require a health declaration, and that is to be expected.

## The usual exclusions

Even when an event meets the definition, there may be no payout. Standard exclusions:

- **intent** — deliberate self-harm;
- **alcohol and substances** — being intoxicated at the time;
- **[hazardous sports](/en/blog/active-sports-insurance-tenerife/)**, unless separately covered;
- **war and civil disturbance**;
- **the consequences of illness**, even where it appeared suddenly.

## The grey areas

**Injury during a blackout.** Someone feels unwell, falls and breaks an arm. The blackout is internal, the fracture external. Assessed case by case, and practice is not uniform.

**Injury at work.** May be covered both by the policy and by the social security system — the important thing is not to confuse the grounds and to file the paperwork correctly.

**A flare-up of an old injury.** If the damage continues an earlier one, the insurer may treat it as a pre-existing condition: [how that works](/en/blog/preexisting-conditions-insurance-spain/).

## How not to get refused

1. **Document the event immediately.** Attending urgencias on the day is the key record. A medical certificate a week later carries far less weight.
2. **Notify the insurer** within the deadline set in the contract.
3. **Keep everything** — reports, scans, invoices and, where relevant, the police report.
4. **Do not invent wording.** Describe what happened.

## What this means in practice

Accident insurance covers **injuries**, not illness. Illness is a [health policy](/en/blog/private-healthcare-tenerife/). Financial protection for the family on death from any cause is [life insurance](/en/blog/life-insurance-spain/), where the definition is broader.

Understanding the boundaries matters more than the sum insured: a policy with a large payout that is not about your risk is of no use.
