After 65 the insurance conversation turns uncomfortable: health policies get more expensive, the health declaration becomes a long list, and in places an age limit appears. But there is one area where the picture is different.

## Why age makes it harder

Health insurance prices the **probability of illness**, and that rises with age. Hence the high tariff, the accumulated [preexistencias](/en/blog/preexisting-conditions-insurance-spain/) and the caution from insurers.

Accident insurance works differently. It prices the **probability of injury**, which does not climb as dramatically with age and, more importantly, does not depend on chronic diagnoses. That is why this is where workable options for older ages exist.

## What is available after 65

The accident insurance range includes products aimed specifically at older ages. Their defining feature is the **absence of medical age loadings**: the tariff does not punish you for not being forty.

What is normally covered:

- **a payout on accidental death** — money to the family, not to a clinic;
- **compensation for disability**, total or partial;
- **related support** after the event.

What is not there: treatment of illness. This is not a substitute for a health policy but a different layer of protection.

## Why it matters in Tenerife specifically

The most common accident at this age is a **fall**. Nothing exotic — a staircase, wet tiles by the pool, an uneven pavement in the old town. The consequences are a hip fracture, a long recovery and costs that land on the family.

If the children live in another country, logistics are added: flights, a carer, help around the house. That is exactly what the payouts compensate.

A related option worth looking at is a [hospital cash policy](/en/blog/hospitalization-cash-insurance-spain/): with a fracture and a long stay it works on precisely those costs.

## How it fits with everything else

The full picture for someone over 65 usually has three parts:

1. **Medicine** — a [health policy](/en/blog/retiree-insurance-spain/) or the public system, and sometimes [Convenio Especial](/en/blog/convenio-especial-vs-private-insurance/) where there is no entitlement to public care.
2. **Accidents** — the subject of this article.
3. **Financial protection for the family** — [life insurance](/en/blog/life-insurance-spain/) and [funeral cover](/en/blog/funeral-insurance-spain/), so costs do not land on the children unexpectedly.

You do not need all of it at once. But it helps to know which layer is covered.

## If you are arranging it for parents

A common scenario: adult children live in Tenerife and the parents come for long stays or follow them out. Here what matters is:

- **age limits** — up to what age the product can be taken out at all, and up to what age it renews;
- **renewal terms** — whether the tariff is repriced sharply;
- **who the beneficiary is** and how quickly payment happens;
- if the parents are still visitors, they also need a [policy for arriving in Spain](/en/blog/travel-insurance-coming-to-spain/).

> Arrange it earlier: the younger the person when the contract starts, the gentler the terms and the wider the choice. This is the one parameter that only gets worse with time.
