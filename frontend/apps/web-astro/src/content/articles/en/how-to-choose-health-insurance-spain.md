There are many policies on the market and, at first glance, they look alike. But the difference is in the details — the ones that show up at the worst moment. Let us go through a simple checklist: what to look at when choosing insurance in Spain and how to switch insurer without losing out.

## What to look at first

- **Copays.** Whether you pay for each appointment. Policies without copays are more convenient and mandatory for visas.
- **Waiting periods (carencias).** When complex services begin (birth, surgery, prosthetics).
- **Cover.** Which specialists, diagnostics and hospitalisation are included.
- **Clinics.** Whether convenient clinics and doctors are available in Tenerife.
- **Language.** Whether there are doctors who speak your language.
- **Certificate.** For a visa, the document for the consulate with the correct wording is mandatory.

## Common traps

- **Looking only at price.** A cheap policy with copays and long waiting periods can cost more in a real situation.
- **Not reading the waiting periods.** Especially important for those planning a birth or an operation.
- **Choosing the wrong policy for a visa.** The consulate will not accept a travel or copay policy.

> Tip: define your situation first — visa, family, health plans — and choose the policy around it. There is no universal "best" policy.

## How to switch insurer

Switching insurer is normal if your current policy does not suit you. Key points:

- **Seniority on waiting periods.** It can often be carried over so you do not serve the carencias again — check this in advance.
- **Timing.** Policies have cancellation terms and renewal dates — worth reviewing.
- **Continuity.** Arrange the new policy so there is no gap in cover, especially if you need it for a visa.
