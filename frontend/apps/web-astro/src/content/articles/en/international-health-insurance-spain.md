Most expats in Tenerife are fine with an ordinary Spanish policy. But if your life is split between countries — you travel home often, work with travel or study abroad — it is worth looking at international health insurance. Let us look at when it is really needed.

## What an international policy is

International health insurance gives **full cover in several countries** — through an international network of providers, not just in Spain. It fits those who are regularly outside the country and want the same access to healthcare everywhere.

## How it differs from an ordinary Spanish policy

- **Ordinary policy:** covers healthcare in Spain and usually only limited assistance on trips abroad (for example, assistance up to €25,000).
- **International policy:** full healthcare in several countries, not just emergency help during a trip.

If you are in Tenerife almost all the time, the difference does not matter. But when living "between two countries", it becomes key.

## Who needs it

- Those who **live between two countries** and spend long periods in each.
- Those who **travel home often** and want to be treated both there and in Spain.
- **Remote workers** with frequent travel.
- **Students and professionals** living temporarily abroad.

## What to look at

- **Geography of cover** — which countries and regions are included.
- **Limits** — annual and for individual services.
- **Evacuation and repatriation** — important for serious cases far from home.
- **Visa compatibility** — if the policy is also needed for residency in Spain.

> Tip: first honestly assess how much time you actually spend outside Spain. An international policy is more expensive, and there is no point overpaying if you mostly live in Tenerife.

## How much it costs

International policies are more expensive than local ones, due to cover in several countries. The exact price is calculated individually — by geography, age and cover. **The estimate is free and commits you to nothing.**
