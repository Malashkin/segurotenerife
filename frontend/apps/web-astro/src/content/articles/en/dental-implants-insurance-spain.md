Dentistry is the part of medicine where Spanish prices surprise people most. A single implant "all in" easily becomes a major purchase, on a par with a holiday. Here is where insurance helps and what not to expect from it.

## Which dental insurance covers implants?

Spanish dental insurance does include implant work, but not for free: the implant is paid at the **network's agreed price**, which you know before treatment starts. What is included at no charge is the basics — on the policies we quote, around 140 procedures: consultations, extractions, emergency care, cleaning, check-ups, X-rays and fluoride treatment.

No dental policy brings an implant down to zero. What differs between plans is how much treatment is included and how much runs with a *franquicia* — in Spanish dental cover, the part you pay yourself at the network rate:

- **Individual.** The basics at no charge; fillings, oral surgery, orthodontics, prosthetics and cosmetic dentistry within the policy, at the agreed price where the treatment calls for it. Access to a network of more than 1,800 clinics across Spain.
- **Family.** The same cover at a single family price, from two members up to four insured people (parents, children and/or grandparents), with a small supplement from the third. A good fit if the children need treatment too: [children's dentistry and orthodontics](/en/blog/childrens-dental-orthodontics-spain/) and [how to insure the whole family in Tenerife](/en/blog/family-insurance-tenerife/).
- **Extended treatments.** A plan that includes more procedures with no co-payment. Worth a look when you already know treatment will be long; exactly what is included is checked in its policy wording.

## How a dental policy is built

It does not work like a health policy. There are **two levels**:

**1. Services at no charge.** First consultation, appointments, X-rays and panoramic scans, professional cleaning, emergency care for acute pain. You attend a network clinic and pay nothing.

**2. Services on a fixed price list.** Everything complex — implants, crowns, prosthetics, orthodontics, surgery — is paid for, but at the **insurer's tariff** rather than the clinic's open rate. That difference is where the value sits.

So the policy does not make an implant free. It makes it **predictably cheaper** and removes the main fear: the price must be quoted upfront, from a list, not "as treatment goes along".

## Price of dental implants with insurance: what it depends on

The final figure is not "the price of an implant" but the total of a treatment plan. What moves it:

- **Number of implants.** Each one is a separate item on the price list.
- **The crown is separate.** The implant is the post in the bone; the visible crown on top is another item, as is the abutment that joins them.
- **Bone preparation.** If there is not enough bone, a graft or sinus lift may be needed first, and that adds to the bill.
- **Diagnostics.** X-rays and a dental CT scan for planning; some of the imaging is usually among the no-charge procedures.
- **Type of prosthesis.** A single crown, a bridge and a full arch on implants are very different jobs.

The difference from a private clinic is predictability. The network price list is fixed item by item and you see it before you start; a private clinic sets its own quote and it can change if the plan changes. To compare, ask the clinic for an itemised treatment plan and put it next to the policy price list, line by line.

## When a policy pays off most

- **Comprehensive treatment.** Several implants, prosthetics, restoration after tooth loss — the saving is most visible here.
- **Families.** Family dental policies exist with a single price covering several insured people, normally better value than individual cover.
- **Planned treatment you already know about.** You can ask for the price list applied to your plan before buying and decide on real numbers.

## Waiting periods and co-payments: where the real limit is

Some dental policies on the market apply a **waiting period** (*carencia*) to complex work: for the first months it is not covered. The logic is the same as in health insurance — [how carencias work in a health policy](/en/blog/carencias-waiting-periods-spain/).

The dental policy we quote has no waiting period: it works **from day one**, prosthetics and implants included. Its real limit is the **co-payment**: complex treatment is not free, it is paid at the network's agreed price. There are no months to wait, but there is no free implant either.

> Before you buy, ask for the policy price list applied to your specific treatment plan: how many items are at no charge and what each of the rest costs. Decide on that number, not on a vague "it's covered".

## Prosthetics: dental insurance, health insurance and the public system

"Prosthesis" means two different things, and each is handled separately.

**A dental prosthesis** — crown, bridge, dentures, implant-supported teeth — belongs to dental insurance. Do not assume health insurance covers your teeth: it almost never does, [dental is a separate line](/en/blog/dental-insurance-spain/) or an add-on.

**A surgical prosthesis** — hip or knee replacement, pacemaker, heart valve, orthopaedic hardware — is health insurance territory and depends on the policy. The expat health policies we quote, for residents and for students, list prosthetics and implants among their cover; with other health policies it comes down to the policy wording. If a specific operation is on your mind, it is checked in the wording when we quote, not assumed.

**Spanish public healthcare.** The common service list of the national health system gives adults dental advice, treatment of acute problems, extractions and early detection of lesions, but [expressly excludes dental implants and prosthetics](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2006-16212) (Royal Decree 1030/2006, annex II, section 9.7), apart from those within the orthoprosthetic benefit. For the wider picture, see [public vs private healthcare in Spain](/en/blog/private-vs-public-healthcare-spain/).
