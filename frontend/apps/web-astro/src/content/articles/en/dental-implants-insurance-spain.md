Dentistry is the part of medicine where Spanish prices surprise people most. A single implant "all in" easily becomes a major purchase, on a par with a holiday. Here is where insurance helps and what not to expect from it.

## How a dental policy is built

It does not work like a health policy. There are **two levels**:

**1. Services at no charge.** First consultation, appointments, X-rays and panoramic scans, professional cleaning, emergency care for acute pain. You attend a network clinic and pay nothing.

**2. Services on a fixed price list.** Everything complex — implants, crowns, prosthetics, orthodontics, surgery — is paid for, but at the **insurer's tariff** rather than the clinic's open rate. That difference is where the value sits.

So the policy does not make an implant free. It makes it **predictably cheaper** and removes the main fear: the price must be quoted upfront, from a list, not "as treatment goes along".

## When a policy pays off most

- **Comprehensive treatment.** Several implants, prosthetics, restoration after tooth loss — the saving is most visible here.
- **Families.** Family dental policies exist with a single price covering several insured people, normally better value than individual cover.
- **Planned treatment you know about in advance.** Precisely the case for taking out the policy several months ahead.

## Waiting periods: the main trap

Complex prosthetics almost always carry a **waiting period**: cover does not start immediately after purchase. The logic is the same as in a health policy — [full explanation of carencias](/en/blog/carencias-waiting-periods-spain/).

Hence the practical rule: **the policy is taken out before treatment is needed**. Buying insurance the week before an implant and claiming the tariff will not work.

> If the tooth is already out and the implant is booked for next month, honestly price both routes. Sometimes it is better to pay for that treatment and take the policy for what comes next, so future work runs on the list.

## What to look at when choosing

1. **The list of no-charge services** — how genuinely broad it is.
2. **The price list for implants and crowns** — it is available before you buy, ask to see it.
3. **The clinic network in Tenerife.** A policy is useless if the nearest network clinic is at the other end of the island — how to check it is covered in the article on [cuadro médico](/en/blog/what-is-cuadro-medico-spain/).
4. **Waiting periods** for each complex item.
5. **Orthodontics** — if braces are needed not only by you but by [a child](/en/blog/childrens-dental-orthodontics-spain/).

## Dentistry and health cover are different things

A common confusion: people assume that having health insurance means teeth are covered too. In most cases they are not — [dentistry is a separate line](/en/blog/dental-insurance-spain/) or an add-on to a health policy. Check this before treatment is needed.
