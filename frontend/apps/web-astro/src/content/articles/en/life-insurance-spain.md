Moving to a new country is always, at heart, about the people we love and the life we are building for them. That is exactly why it is worth thinking about what would happen to your family if one day you were no longer there. This is not a dark subject — it is about care.

## Care that is felt later, not today

Life insurance is rarely on your mind on an ordinary day. Yet it quietly stands behind your family — like a promise that, in a hard moment, the people closest to you will not be left on their own.

For expats this matters even more. In Spain there is often no large family nearby, no familiar support to turn to. A life policy becomes the safety net that relatives and home once were.

## Life insurance in Tenerife: what the island actually changes

Worth saying plainly: there is no separate Canarian version of life insurance. The rules are national — the Spanish insurance contract act applies the same way in Santa Cruz as in Madrid, and the cover an insurer offers on the mainland is the cover it offers here. What changes is not the policy but **the situation of the person buying it on the island**:

- **Your beneficiaries usually live in another country.** Parents in Manchester, a sister in Chicago, children back home. That is why it matters to **name your beneficiaries in the policy by name**, rather than leaving a generic "my heirs". It speeds up the claim and avoids arguments at a distance, when the paperwork is in Spanish.
- **Your family needs to know the policy exists.** Spain keeps a national register of death-cover insurance contracts at the Ministry of Justice, where relatives can check whether someone held a life policy. A useful backstop — but it works far better if you have also told someone you trust.
- **The mortgage was signed in Spain.** The property is here, the debt is here, and the instalments keep running whatever happens.
- **Inheritance and tax follow Spanish rules.** A life insurance payout falls under Spanish inheritance and gift tax, which is devolved to the regions: the Canary Islands apply their own reliefs and allowances, different from other parts of Spain. That is not a footnote when your beneficiaries are non-residents.

Beyond that, it is standard Spanish cover. If someone offers you "special island cover", that is marketing, not a policy clause.

## When there is a mortgage or a loan

Many expats on Tenerife buy a home with a mortgage. Here life insurance plays a very human role.

- If something happens, the policy can help clear the home loan.
- Your family can **stay in their home** instead of selling it in a rush.
- Loved ones do not inherit anxiety and monthly payments along with their grief.

There is a version built exactly for this, **capital decreciente** or decreasing cover: the sum insured falls as you pay the loan down. We go through it in the guide to [life insurance tied to a Spanish mortgage](/en/blog/mortgage-life-insurance-spain/) — including the part banks rarely volunteer: you are not obliged to buy the policy from the lender.

> The home you dreamed of together should stay a home — not turn into an obligation that weighs everyone down.

## Securing your family's future: what they actually receive

A life policy secures your family's future in a very concrete way: **if you die, the insurer pays a cash sum to the people you named**, and that money is theirs to use as they see fit. Nobody dictates how. They can clear the mortgage, cover day-to-day costs while they reorganise, or simply take the time not to decide anything in a hurry.

This is where the most common mix-up happens, and it is worth untangling. A **family health policy** — paediatrician, specialists, maternity, scans — looks after your family *while you are here*: it pays for doctors and treatment. That is what we cover in the guide to [family insurance in Tenerife](/en/blog/family-insurance-tenerife/). A **life policy** works in the opposite scenario: it pays nothing towards doctors, it pays your family when you are gone. Different jobs entirely, which is why plenty of households hold both.

## Life or decesos: which one you are buying

Short answer: **a life policy hands your beneficiaries money; a decesos policy arranges and pays for the funeral**. They are not alternatives and neither replaces the other.

- **Life (vida).** A capital sum paid to whoever you nominate. It goes on the mortgage, on school fees, on keeping the household running.
- **Funeral (decesos).** A service: the funeral director, transport, administrative formalities, legal advice and support for the family. For expats the decisive item is usually repatriation — expensive and hard to organise from grief and from abroad. We set it out in the article on [funeral insurance in Spain](/en/blog/funeral-insurance-spain/).

In practice, people with a mortgage or young children start with life cover; people living far from their home country and worried about repatriation start with decesos.

## The kinds of protection there are

In plain terms, cover is usually built around a few needs:

- **Support for the family** — a payment to your loved ones so they can keep their way of life.
- **Protecting the home** — help clearing a mortgage or a large loan.
- **Extra support** — many policies also account for permanent disability, when you are the one who needs support.

The exact mix is shaped around your family, the age of your children and your commitments — there is no single template for everyone, which is why the price is quoted case by case.

## What a life policy does not cover

Leaving this part out would do you no favours, because this is where expectations break:

- **The health declaration decides everything.** You answer a health questionnaire, the *declaración de salud*, when you apply. Hiding a condition you knew about can reduce the payout or void it. That is not small print — it is written into Spanish insurance contract law.
- **Suicide has a time limit.** Spanish law covers it once a year has passed since the policy was signed, unless the contract says otherwise.
- **Risky jobs and sports must be declared.** Working at height, diving, paragliding — not automatically excluded, but they change the premium or need a specific clause. Declaring them protects your family; hiding them leaves it exposed.
- **There are age limits**, both to take out a policy and to keep it running.

No insurer promises in advance that it will accept you: there is an underwriting process, and the answer depends on your age, your health and the sum you ask for.

We move for a better life — the sun, the sea, a feeling of freedom. Life insurance simply adds confidence to that: whatever happens, the people we love will be protected.
