The premium went up, the clinic network is inconvenient, or you simply found something better. You can change insurer in Spain — but it is easy to lose either a year's payment or the time you have already accrued. Here is the order of operations.

## The key rule: the contract is annual

Spanish health policies run for a year and **renew automatically**. Do nothing and a new paid term begins.

To prevent that you must **notify the insurer in writing that you are not renewing, as a rule no later than one month before** the current term ends. The exact deadline is in your own contract terms — check it.

## Step by step

**1. Find the renewal date.** It is on the policy or in your online account. The notice period counts back from it.

**2. Write the request.** A short text is enough: policyholder details, policy number, a clear statement that you are not renewing, and the date the contract ends.

**3. Send it so that proof remains.** The online account with a delivery record, email with a read receipt, or registered post (burofax) — the most robust option if it is ever disputed. A phone call proves nothing.

**4. Wait for confirmation** from the insurer and keep it.

**5. Check the direct debits.** Cancel the bank mandate **only after** you have that confirmation — otherwise you do not get a cancellation, you get an unpaid premium.

> One special case: if the policy was sold at a distance, there is normally a short window right after signing in which you can withdraw without giving a reason. The term is in the conditions.

## How not to lose your accrued time

The most valuable thing in your old policy is the **[waiting periods](/en/blog/carencias-waiting-periods-spain/) you have already served**. A new insurer will often credit them, so major services work from day one.

For that you need to:

- ask the previous insurer for a **certificate of continuous cover**;
- start the new policy **with no gap** — it begins the day the old one ends;
- hand the certificate to the new insurer when you apply.

If there is even a few weeks' pause between policies, the accrued time is likely lost and every carencia restarts.

## Before you cancel

- **The health declaration is filled in again.** Anything new becomes a [preexistencia](/en/blog/preexisting-conditions-insurance-spain/) in the new policy. Sometimes staying put is the better deal.
- **Your age has changed.** The new tariff is priced on your current age — [what drives the price](/en/blog/health-insurance-cost-spain/).
- **The clinic network.** Check the new insurer's [cuadro médico](/en/blog/what-is-cuadro-medico-spain/) specifically for Tenerife, not for Spain as a whole.
- **Residency policies.** If the insurance supports your immigration file, a gap in cover is not an option — renewal and the [certificate](/en/blog/insurance-certificate-for-visa/) have their own requirements.

## Common mistakes

1. Giving notice two weeks ahead — too late, the contract has renewed.
2. Cancelling the bank mandate instead of the contract — and ending up in debt.
3. Cancelling the old policy and buying the new one "next month, when there is time" — losing the accrued periods.
4. Switching for a small saving and losing cover for a condition that appeared during the year.
