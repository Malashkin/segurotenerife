You take out a policy, pay the first premium — and a month later find out the operation you had planned is not covered yet. The reason is the **carencia**, or waiting period. It is one of the main causes of disappointment with Spanish insurance, and it can almost always be avoided if you know the rules in advance.

## What a waiting period is

A carencia is a period from the moment the policy takes effect during which **part of the cover is not yet active**. You pay your premiums, the policy is live, but specific items switch on later.

The insurer's logic is easy to follow: without it, people would buy a policy a week before a planned operation and cancel it straight after.

## What usually falls under a carencia

The exact list is always in the terms of your own policy, but the typical picture looks like this:

- **Available immediately:** GP and specialist appointments, basic diagnostics, [telemedicine](/en/blog/private-healthcare-tenerife/), emergency care after an accident.
- **Subject to a waiting period:** planned hospitalisation and surgery, [pregnancy and childbirth](/en/blog/maternity-insurance-spain/), complex [dentistry and prosthetics](/en/blog/dental-insurance-spain/), some expensive scans.

The lengths differ by service: a few months for planned procedures, considerably longer for childbirth — which is why a policy is taken out well before a pregnancy is planned.

## Why a residency policy must have none

Consulates and immigration authorities require the insurance to cover you **from day one and in full**. A policy with waiting periods does not meet that.

That is why [visa and residency policies](/en/blog/insurance-for-residency-visa/) are issued **with no co-payments and no waiting periods** — they are a separate product line. If someone offers you a standard domestic tariff saying "this will do too", that is a rejection risk.

> Check the wording on the certificate: it must state explicitly that there are no carencias and no copagos. More on this in the article about the [certificate for the consulate](/en/blog/insurance-certificate-for-visa/).

## How to avoid a waiting period

**1. Take out a residency policy.** By definition it has no carencias.

**2. Carry your time over when switching.** If you already held a valid policy, the new insurer will often credit the time already served instead of making you start again. You need a certificate of prior cover — [how to arrange it](/en/blog/how-to-switch-health-insurance-spain/).

**3. Start early.** The simplest option: if you know that treatment or a baby is a year away, start the policy now and the waiting periods will be behind you by then.

## The common mistake

People cancel the old policy, wait a couple of months, then buy a cheaper one. The accrued time is lost and every waiting period restarts from zero. If you plan to change insurer, do it **with no gap** between the dates.
