When you take out private health insurance in Spain, it most often works through the **cuadro médico** — the insurer's network of doctors and clinics. For new residents in Tenerife this is a key concept, because it decides which doctors you can see. Let us look at it in plain terms.

## What the cuadro médico is

The cuadro médico is the **list of doctors, specialists and clinics** included in your policy. You choose any of them and book directly. The visit is usually **with no payment on the spot** — or with a small copay if your policy has one.

Essentially it is the "map" of your healthcare: GPs, specialists, labs and hospitals available through the insurance.

## How to use it

1. Open your insurer's cuadro médico directory.
2. Filter by province (for Tenerife, Santa Cruz de Tenerife) and specialty.
3. Choose a doctor or clinic and book.
4. At the visit show your policy — payment goes through the insurer.

## What to check in Tenerife

- **Whether there are enough clinics nearby** — so you do not cross the whole island.
- **The specialists you need** — cardiologist, paediatrician, gynaecologist, etc. within reach.
- **Language** — whether there are doctors who speak your language.
- **Hospitals** — which clinics are available for hospitalisation.

> Tip: check the cuadro médico **before** buying the policy. That is where the difference between two seemingly identical policies lies — one has a rich network in Tenerife, the other almost empty.

## Network or reimbursement

- **Network policy (cuadro médico):** only doctors from the list, but the visit is with no payment on the spot.
- **Reimbursement policy (reembolso):** any doctor, but you pay first and are refunded afterwards.

Many policies combine both formats — you can use the network and reimbursement.

## How much it costs

Network policies are usually more affordable than reimbursement ones. The exact price is calculated individually — by age, cover and family. **The estimate is free and commits you to nothing.**
