Expecting a child in a new country is doubly exciting. The good news: Tenerife has quality private healthcare, and insurance helps you go through pregnancy and birth calmly, without big bills. But here everything hinges on one thing — when you take out the policy.

## The key rule: arrange it early

The main feature of maternity insurance is the **waiting period**. Birth cover does not start immediately, but after a set time from taking out the policy (often around 8–10 months). That is why the policy should be arranged **before** pregnancy — so that by the time of birth, cover is already active.

If you are already pregnant, birth under a new policy most likely will not be covered. But that does not mean there are no options — prenatal care and some services can be considered separately.

## What cover usually includes

A full policy with maternity typically includes:

- prenatal care and check-ups with a gynaecologist;
- ultrasounds and scheduled tests;
- specialist consultations;
- birth in a private clinic;
- postnatal follow-up and first care for the baby.

## What to look out for

- **Waiting period** — check it in advance; it decides when to take out the policy.
- **Clinics** — check which maternity units and specialists are available in Tenerife under the policy.
- **Baby cover** — ask how and when the newborn can be added to the policy.
- **Language** — where possible, doctors who speak your language: at a moment like this it matters especially.

> Tip: if you are still planning a child, this is the ideal time to take out the policy. That way you calmly pass the waiting period and reach pregnancy with cover already in force.
